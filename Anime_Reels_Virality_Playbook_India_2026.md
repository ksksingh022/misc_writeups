## 1.2 The three levers that actually decide your reach

Adam Mosseri has confirmed the ranking signals. Everything else is noise.

**1. WATCH TIME (heaviest signal)**
- Instagram moved from counting 3-second views to **total watch time + replay rate**.
- A 15s reel watched 3 times (45s total) now outranks a 60s reel watched once.
- Reels with completion below **~35%** get capped at their initial test audience and die there.
- **→ Your 30s cap is an advantage. Push most reels to 12–22s and make them loop.**

**2. SENDS PER REACH (the growth engine)**
- DM shares are weighted **3–5x higher than likes** and carry the most weight for *unconnected* reach — i.e. reaching people who don't follow you.
- For a new page, unconnected reach is 100% of your growth. Target **70%+ of reach from non-followers**.
- **→ Every single story below is engineered around one question: "who does the viewer immediately want to send this to?"**

**3. LIKES PER REACH**
- Still counts, weakest of the three. Don't design for it.

**Supporting mechanics:**
- **Hook rate:** ~60% of viewers must survive past 3 seconds. Below 50% hook rate, the reel is dead on arrival. Your first frame must carry a visual + a text line, not a slow fade-in.
- **Hashtags are filing labels, not growth levers.** Some data shows hashtag-heavy posts get *lower* reach than clean ones. Use **3–5, specific**.
- **Keywords in the caption drive ~30% more reach.** Instagram reads your caption as search text. This is where your real SEO lives.
- **Watermarks = instant disqualification** from Explore/Reels recommendation. No CapCut logo, no TikTok logo, no AI-tool watermark. Ever.
- **Original audio vs trending audio:** use trending audio for discovery pushes, original/ambient for emotional stories where the sound *is* the atmosphere. Don't force trending audio onto a sad story — it kills the emotion and the completion rate.

## 1.3 Your posting protocol

**Frequency:** 4–7 reels/week. Consistency beats bursts. Space them ≥24h apart.

**India windows (IST):**
- **Primary:** 8:00–11:00 AM
- **Primary:** 7:00–10:00 PM ← best for entertainment/emotional content
- **Best days:** Wednesday and Thursday. Weakest: Friday/Saturday.
- Post at **8:15 PM or 9:10 PM**, not 8:00/9:00 sharp — everyone in your niche posts on the round hour and you're competing with all of them at once.

**First-hour protocol (this is where reels are won or lost):**
1. Post → immediately share to your Story with a "which one hits harder" style poll or sticker.
2. Reply to **every** comment in the first 30 minutes. Instagram now tracks *conversation depth*, not just comment count — a real 3-message back-and-forth is worth far more than 20 emoji replies.
3. Check the 60-minute view count. If it's flat, it will stay flat. Don't delete it — just learn and move on.
4. Never delete a flop. Deleting resets nothing and looks like account instability.

**Metrics that matter — track only these four:**
| Metric | Target | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Hook rate (3s retention) | >60% | Insights → Retention graph |
| Completion / replay rate | >50% | Insights |
| Sends ÷ Reach | >1.5% is strong | Insights |
| Non-follower reach % | >70% | Insights |

## 1.4 Content system, not content luck

The 50 stories below are organised into **8 recurring series**. This is deliberate.

Instagram's 2026 model rewards **topical consistency** — accounts that build a recognisable community around a specific thing. Random one-off reels train the algorithm on nothing. A named series trains it fast, and it gives viewers a reason to follow rather than just watch.

Put the series name as the first text line on the reel and in the caption. When a viewer sees the same series name three times, you stop being a random reel and become a page.

## 1.5 Safety and IP guardrails (read this once, follow it always)

- **All 50 stories below are original.** No existing anime characters, no Gojo, no Naruto, no Nezuko. Beyond the legal issue, IP-based content also gets you competing directly with the reposter swamp — which is exactly the pool you want to stay out of.
- **Style ≠ character.** "90s shojo pastel palette" or "modern shonen action lighting" is a legitimate aesthetic direction. "Make it look like Demon Slayer's Tanjiro" is not.
- **No sensitive content.** Nothing below involves self-harm, real violence against people, gore, religion-baiting, caste, politics, communal themes, or anything sexualised. This is not just an ethics point: Instagram's recommendation guidelines silently strip Explore eligibility from borderline content, which is the exact reach you need.
- **Children in stories are shown as children** — playing, studying, being loved by family. Never styled, never framed for attention.
- **Add captions/text overlays.** Most Indian viewers scroll on mute. If the story only works with sound, it doesn't work.
- **Disclose AI where the platform asks you to.** Label it. Audiences in 2026 are fine with AI content that's honest and hostile to AI content that pretends.

**Audio:** what the sound should do
**Why it goes viral:** tied to a real algorithm signal, not vibes

**Why these fields and not others:**
- *Hook text* exists because your 3-second retention is the gate to everything.
- *Closing text* exists because sends happen at the emotional peak, which is the last 2 seconds.
- *Loop point* exists because replay rate is now counted inside watch time. A seamless loop can double your effective watch time on a 15s reel with zero extra production.
- *Sends to* exists because "who does this get sent to" is the single highest-leverage question in the 2026 algorithm, and most creators never ask it.

## 4.2 The caption formula

Instagram reads your caption as search text. Keywords in captions drive roughly **30% more reach**. Hashtags do not. This is the single most under-used lever in the entire niche.

**Structure — first line is everything:**

```
[Line 1: the emotional hook, ~8 words, this is what shows before "more"]
[Line 2: one keyword-loaded descriptive sentence for Instagram's search index]
[Line 3: series name + "original AI anime story"]
[Line 4: the send CTA]
[3–5 hashtags]
```

**Keyword bank — work these into line 2 naturally:**
`anime story` · `emotional anime short` · `anime short film` · `indian anime` · `hindi anime story` · `original anime` · `AI anime` · `anime edit` · `sad anime story` · `anime love story` · `aesthetic anime` · `animation short` · `desi anime`

Don't stuff. Two or three per caption, in a sentence a human would actually write.

**Send CTAs, ranked by observed effectiveness:**
1. "Send this to [specific person type]" — best. Removes both the *whether* and the *who* decision.
2. "Watch it twice" — manufactures replays directly.
3. "Save this for [specific moment]" — converts a view into a save.
4. "Which one would you pick?" — comment driver.
5. ❌ "Follow for more" — dead. Doesn't work, mildly suppresses.

## 4.5 Review-day audit (Day 15, then every 14 days)

Open Insights and fill this in for your last 14 reels. Only these four numbers matter.

| Metric | Kill threshold | Healthy | Elite |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3s hook rate | <50% | 60–70% | >75% |
| Completion/replay | <35% (reel is capped, dies) | 50–65% | >70% |
| Sends ÷ Reach | <0.5% | 1–2% | >2.5% |
| Non-follower reach | <50% | 70% | >85% |

**Diagnosis:**
- **Low hook, everything else fine** → your first frame is weak. Same story, new opening. Don't rewrite the story.
- **Good hook, low completion** → your middle sags, or it's too long. Cut 4 seconds from the middle.
- **Good completion, low sends** → the story is enjoyable but not *about* anyone. Every send happens because the viewer thought of a specific person. Rewrite the closing text to name that person type.
- **Everything good, low reach** → you're being throttled for something mechanical: a watermark, an external-audio issue, or posting too fast after the previous reel. Check the file, not the story.

## 4.6 Scaling what works

When a reel clears 3x your account average, do all three of these within 7 days:

1. **Remake it.** Same story, different characters, different setting, different season. Not a repost — a genuine second version. Instagram's originality detection punishes reposts, not variations on your own theme.
